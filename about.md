### Glossary

- **BED** (biologically effective dose): a dose adjusted for fraction size, $n d (1 + d/(\alpha/\beta))$, minus any repopulation term. Gy₁₀ and Gy₃ mean BED computed with α/β = 10 Gy and 3 Gy. On the Uncertainty tab, where tumor α/β varies from draw to draw, BED differences are in Gy at each draw's α/β.
- **EQD2**: the dose in 2-Gy fractions with the same BED.
- **α/β**: the tissue's sensitivity to fraction size (Gy). Low for late-responding tissues and the spinal cord, about 10 Gy for tumor and acute mucosa.
- **DI and DD models**: two descriptions of accelerated tumor repopulation. DI (dose-independent): a fixed onset day and rate. DD (dose-dependent): onset and rate depend on how fast tumor cells are being killed.
- **Comparison regimen**: the regimen every difference (Δ) is taken against. One comparison is used on every tab and named in the banner.
- **Tumor threshold**: the smallest tumor BED change from the comparison that counts as meeting the tumor criterion. Negative values allow a loss.
- **Draw**: one joint set of model parameters from the Bayesian refit to randomized trials. A share of draws is not a probability of tumor control or of safety.
- **OAR** (organ at risk) and **constraint**: a dose limit for a normal organ, stated for a given fractionation. Maximum-dose limits are converted here with an organ α/β. Mean-dose limits are compared on physical dose.

### What this calculator does

For a radiotherapy regimen of $n$ fractions of $d$ Gy given over $T$ days, it computes three biologically effective doses (BEDs):

- the tumor BED for head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), under two models of tumor repopulation
- the BED for early (acute mucosal) toxicity
- the BED for late normal-tissue toxicity.

Every tab compares the evaluated regimen (by default 18 × 2.9 Gy, 52.2 Gy in 24 days) with one comparison (control) regimen chosen in the sidebar, by default 35 × 2 Gy in 47 days. HYPNO's two arms can also be chosen. The both-model screen uses the same comparison unless it is explicitly overridden there. Toxicity limits set as "comparison plus allowed excess" are relative to that comparison. The HYPNO, Fowler and Fenwick limits are fixed values. Each analysis tab states its comparison, DD anchor and numerical limits at the top. Gy₁₀ and Gy₃ denote BED computed with α/β = 10 Gy and 3 Gy.

The tab "Search" lists shorter, hypofractionated regimens whose tumor BED change from the comparison meets a chosen minimum (which may be negative, an allowed loss of modeled tumor BED), while the early and late toxicity BEDs stay within chosen limits. The "Both-model screen" tab lists regimens that meet the threshold under both tumor models, with their range over selected parameter settings. The "Sensitivity" tab shows how the tumor BED difference of the regimen being evaluated changes across tumor parameter sets.

This is a research and teaching tool, meant for hypothesis generation. It is not a treatment-planning system and must not be used on its own to choose treatment for a patient. Its parameter uncertainty (Uncertainty tab) comes from a refit to summary trial data and from stated priors, and a tumor-control change is shown only conditional on a control rate you enter. Any regimen considered for a trial needs clinical precedent, organ-at-risk planning and toxicity stopping rules.

### Tumor BED, dose-independent (DI) repopulation model

Accelerated repopulation starts at a fixed time $T_k$ and proceeds at a fixed rate $\lambda_{DI}$ (the Withers "hockey stick"). It costs $\lambda_{DI}/\alpha$ Gy of tumor BED per day after $T_k$. An optional background growth rate $g$ (default 0) costs $g/\alpha$ Gy per day over the whole course:

$$BED_{DI} = n d \left(1 + \frac{d}{\alpha/\beta}\right) - \frac{\lambda_{DI}\,\max(0,\ T - T_k)}{\alpha} - \frac{g\,T}{\alpha}$$

### Tumor BED, dose-dependent (DD) repopulation model

Repopulation is a response to cell killing:

- It starts when the cumulative log cell kill reaches a constant $C$.
- Its rate is $\lambda_{DD}$ times the mean fraction of tumor cells killed per day.

With $K = n d (1 + d/(\alpha/\beta))$:

$$BED_{DD} = K - \frac{\lambda_{DD}\left(1 - e^{-\alpha K / T}\right)\max\left(0,\ T - \frac{C\,T}{\alpha K}\right)}{\alpha} - \frac{g\,T}{\alpha}$$

**Calibration at a fixed anchor.** $C$ is not entered by the user. It is solved so that $BED_{DD} = BED_{DI}$ for the **anchor regimen** (default 35 × 2 Gy in 47 days), for whatever tumor parameters are entered. The anchor is separate from the comparison regimen: choosing another comparison, such as HYPNO's control arm, changes the differences shown but not the DD model. Version 1 calibrated at a fixed 35 × 2 Gy, 47-day course, but with a constant that is correct only at tumor α/β 10 Gy (see Version below). Version 2.0 calibrated at the comparison regimen, so changing the comparison changed the DD model itself. A version 2.0 result for a once-daily Monday-to-Friday comparison is approximated by entering that comparison's fractions and dose as the anchor. The anchor's overall time always comes from that calendar, so a comparison with another overall time cannot be reproduced exactly: HYPNO's control arm (33 fractions in 38 days at 6 per week) becomes 45 days as an anchor. The DI parameters ($\lambda_{DI}$, $T_k$) still set the DD calibration, and the shared $g$ cancels in it.

For some parameter combinations no repopulation onset after the start of treatment can make the two models agree at the anchor. This happens, for example, when $\lambda_{DD}$ is too small to produce the DI repopulation. The DD model is then marked as unavailable and its values are hidden. Searches that require or rank by DD return no regimens with an explanation, and the "DI or DD" criterion with ranking by the larger gain uses DI alone. The default both-model criterion and smaller-change ranking then return no regimens, with an explanation. With $\lambda_{DI}$ = 0, neither model has accelerated repopulation.

**How this DD model relates to the published one.** The equations follow Shuryak, Hall and Brenner [1] (Eq. 1–5). The parameters do not. In [1], each model was fitted separately to 35 regimens from 16 publications (7283 patients; α/β 10 Gy, $g$ = 0.0154 per day), and the DD fit had its own α (0.224 Gy⁻¹), maximum rate (1.17 per day) and $C$ (14.5). Here, both models share one α and $C$ is calibrated to the DI model. The two versions differ in practice. With the fitted parameters of [1], the DD model places repopulation in the 35 × 2 Gy course from about day 36 and gives small tumor BED gains for the GORTEC (+3.2 Gy₁₀) and TROG (+2.1 Gy₁₀) accelerated arms and about no change for HYPNO (+0.5 Gy₁₀). With the calibrated version and this calculator's presets, the DD model predicts losses for all three. The fitted values are not offered as defaults. DD results here are therefore a calibrated variant of the dose-dependent hypothesis, not a reproduction of the fitted model.

### Early toxicity BED (acute oral and pharyngeal mucosa)

Mucosa repopulates quickly after an onset time $T_{k,e}$, at rate $\lambda_e = \ln 2 / T_{p,e}$:

$$BED_{early} = n d \left(1 + \frac{d}{(\alpha/\beta)_e}\right) - \frac{\lambda_e\,\max(0,\ T_e - T_{k,e})}{\alpha_e}$$

Here $T_e$ is the overall time in **elapsed days** (last treatment day minus first), which is the calendar count minus 1. Fowler's parameters and tolerance zone were derived on that clock [5, 9]: 70 Gy in 35 fractions over 7 weeks counts as 46 days and gives 53.1 Gy₁₀, as in his Table 1. The calculator converts once, inside the calculation.

### Late toxicity BED

Late-responding tissues are assumed not to repopulate during treatment, so $\alpha$ does not enter:

$$BED_{late} = n d \left(1 + \frac{d}{(\alpha/\beta)_l}\right)$$

It is evaluated at the prescription dose per fraction, not at the dose to individual organs at risk. The Results tab also shows it at α/β 2 and 4 Gy, because one α/β can reverse a comparison: 20 × 2.9 Gy is below 35 × 2 Gy at α/β 3 Gy (114.1 vs 116.7 Gy₃) but above it at α/β 2 Gy (142.1 vs 140.0). Equivalent doses in 2-Gy fractions are $EQD2 = BED / (1 + 2/(\alpha/\beta))$.

### How the regimen search works

- **Candidates.** Once-daily treatment, 5 (Monday to Friday) or 6 (Monday to Saturday) fractions per week as chosen for the evaluated regimen, starting on the chosen day. Days without treatment are not applied to candidates. The number of fractions runs over the chosen range, below the comparison number. The dose per fraction runs over multiples of the chosen step (default 0.05 Gy) above the comparison dose per fraction, up to the chosen maximum (default 5 Gy). Rows above 3.1 Gy per fraction, the largest fraction size among the benchmark regimens shown (HYHOPE, in favorable-risk HPV-positive oropharynx cancer), are flagged "above benchmark fraction sizes". The flag describes that set of regimens only. A regimen without the flag does not thereby have precedent in another population, with other chemotherapy or other volumes.
- **Time.** The overall time must be at least the chosen number of days shorter than the comparison (default 7 days).
- **Tumor.** The tumor BED change from the comparison must be at least the chosen minimum (default +1 Gy) under the chosen model: both (default), DI or DD, DI only, or DD only. Requiring both avoids listing a regimen by default when it meets the threshold under only one of these model assumptions. It does not establish clinical benefit or account for all model uncertainty. "DI or DD" requires the threshold under at least one evaluable model and can include regimens that fail it under the other. A negative minimum allows a loss of modeled tumor BED. It is a screening value: a clinical non-inferiority margin concerns a defined outcome at a defined time and needs its own statistical justification. The column "Credited by" says which model meets the criterion.
- **Early toxicity.** Choose one of four limits. The default is the early BED of HYPNO's 55 Gy in 20 fractions (55.9 Gy₁₀ with the default acute parameters), computed with the same acute model as the candidates. That regimen was given to 395 patients in a randomized trial, 75% with weekly cisplatin, with grade 3+ acute mucositis in 50.9%. The limit therefore means "modeled acute BED no higher than a regimen with known randomized acute toxicity", not "safe". The others are the comparison's early BED plus an allowed excess, the lower edge of the Fowler zone (59 Gy₁₀ for radiotherapy alone, or 54 Gy₁₀ with concurrent chemotherapy), and the Fenwick et al. boundary [15] on cell-kill BED (α/β 10 Gy, no repopulation term), $69.5\,(T/32.2)/\sin(T/32.2) - 3.5$ Gy₁₀ with $T$ in inclusive days and the angle in radians, defined only up to 50 days. It is an empirical boundary from historical radiotherapy-alone schedules: all schedules below it were tolerable in that data set, which is not a guarantee for new schedules, and chemotherapy is not included. It does not depend on Fowler's parameters. Meade et al. [16] advise careful phase 1 testing of a regimen that at least two of three acute models predict to be intolerable.
- **Why HYPNO is the default acute limit.** It is anchored to observed acute toxicity in a randomized trial in the target setting (chemoradiation for locally advanced HNSCC), whereas Fowler's 59 Gy₁₀ zone was derived for radiotherapy alone. Its limits: HYPNO's population (no HPV testing, 87% tobacco users, 12 centers), its weekly cisplatin 35 mg/m² (not 3-weekly 100 mg/m²) and its target volumes may differ from a new trial's; regimens much shorter than 26 days rely on the mucosal repopulation model for the time dependence; and similar modeled acute BEDs do not ensure similar toxicity (see the HYHOPE example below). A candidate at this limit still needs acute-toxicity monitoring and stopping rules.
- **Late toxicity.** Choose the comparison's late BED plus an allowed excess (default 0), or the late BED of HYPNO's 55 Gy in 20 fractions (105.4 Gy₃ at α/β 3 Gy). The default comparison allows more: 70 Gy in 35 fractions is 116.7 Gy₃.
- **Validity.** One rule applies to the search, the Sensitivity tab, the both-model screen, the report card and the Uncertainty tab: a tumor model's difference is used only when the model is calibrated and both the comparison and the regimen BED are positive. Otherwise it is shown as not evaluable, and it never counts as meeting a criterion.
- **Output.** For each number of fractions, the dose per fraction that ranks highest is listed. The ranking is by the smaller change of the two models (default, conservative), the larger change (optimistic), or one model's change. A row is ranked only when its ranking value can be evaluated: the smaller change needs both models' differences, a one-model ranking needs that model's, and the larger change uses whichever differences are evaluable. A regimen that meets every limit but cannot be ranked is not listed. It stays in the download with rank_eligible FALSE. The table also shows fractions saved and how far each regimen is below its early and late limits (limit minus regimen). The ZIP download also holds every qualifying regimen, not only the listed one, and a scenario file that reproduces the search. When organ doses are entered as a percentage of the prescription on the Organs at risk tab, and its box is ticked, every candidate must also stay within each organ limit (a converted maximum-dose constraint or a physical mean-dose limit). The both-model screen has its own download, which also holds every listed regimen under every selected parameter setting.
- **Replay.** A scenario file is rerun exactly only by a calculator with the same schema, equation, selection and scenario-set versions, hinge and clocks. Version 3 writes schema 3 and also replays files of versions 2.6 and 2.7 exactly, because their calibrated DD equations are unchanged. Other files are refused. A file from an older version can be reanalyzed with the current rules (`migrate_scenario()`), which records the original versions and ID and gives the new scenario its own ID. That is a reanalysis, not a replay, and its results may differ: for example version 2.6 changed which regimen the conservative ranking lists when one model's BED is not positive.
- **Next step.** The column "Next dose step would exceed" names what one more dose step would break: early toxicity, late toxicity, an organ-at-risk limit, or the tumor criterion (or a combination). "End of dose search range" means the maximum dose searched was reached. "None (a higher dose ranks lower)" means a higher dose would still meet every limit but ranks lower, which can happen under the DD model.

With the defaults (Scenario D18, HYPNO acute limit, both models, minimum change +1 Gy and smaller-change ranking), no candidate regimen meets all limits. If the criterion is explicitly changed to "DI or DD model" and the ranking to the larger change, three regimens meet +1 Gy under DI only: 29 × 2.25 Gy in 39 days (ΔDI +1.19, ΔDD −2.41 Gy₁₀), 28 × 2.30 Gy in 38 days (+1.13, −2.86) and 30 × 2.20 Gy in 40 days (+1.12, −2.05). None gains under the DD model, so the two models disagree about these regimens. They are DI-only hypotheses. Their acute BEDs (55.2–55.4 Gy₁₀) are below the HYPNO limit but above that of 35 × 2 Gy. With both models and an explicitly allowed loss of 3 Gy, the same three regimens are listed in the order 30, 29, 28 fractions. Regimens whose modeled changes both meet an allowed loss against HYPNO are found by choosing HYPNO as the comparison and a negative threshold (or by overriding them on the both-model screen). The list depends strongly on the tumor repopulation settings. An empty list is a result, not an error.

### Both-model screen (two-model agreement)

The tab "Both-model screen" lists regimens whose tumor BED change from the comparison regimen is at least the tumor threshold under DI and under DD, within the toxicity limits chosen on the Search tab, saving at least one day by default. It uses the global comparison and threshold unless they are overridden on the tab (shown highlighted). The examples below use HYPNO's 55 Gy in 20 fractions as the comparison and −3 Gy as the threshold. A toxicity limit set relative to the comparison then means "no higher than HYPNO", for late BED as well as early. The allowed change is a BED value applied at each setting's own tumor α/β, so −3 Gy corresponds to −2.33, −2.50 and −2.65 Gy EQD2 at α/β 7, 10 and 15 Gy. The DD anchor is the same as on the other tabs.

For each regimen the tab shows:

- ΔBED under each model with the current parameters, and the range of each over the 15 selected parameter settings of the Sensitivity tab (five tumor presets, tumor α/β 7, 10 and 15 Gy)
- in how many evaluable settings both changes meet the allowed change (the toxicity limits do not depend on the tumor setting). A setting is evaluable when both models' differences can be evaluated. It is not evaluable when a comparison or regimen BED is not positive, or the DD model cannot be calibrated, and it is then counted separately, never as passing. Both ranges are the minimum and maximum over the same evaluable settings as the count, not confidence intervals. The download also gives each model's own count of evaluable settings (n_valid_di, n_valid_dd) and every setting's values
- the DD repopulation onset day, and whether the course ends before the DI onset.

The last two help explain why the models agree or differ. Under DI, accelerated repopulation is nearly absent in a course that ends well before the onset day, so shortening such a course further saves little of it (the smooth threshold leaves a small tail). Background growth $g$, if set, still gives a whole-course gain of $g/\alpha$ per day saved: for 17 × 3.05 Gy against HYPNO under D18, $g$ = 0.0154 per day changes ΔDI from −2.46 to −2.33 Gy₁₀, although both courses end before day 28. Under DD, onset and rate depend on cell killing; with the defaults repopulation in these short courses starts around days 14–16, so shortening saves part of it. A shorter course does not lose tumor BED under every setting: with tumor α/β 2 Gy (not a recommended HNSCC value) the same 17 × 3.05 Gy gains +0.30 Gy under DI at an acute BED below HYPNO's.

With HYPNO as the comparison, −3 Gy and otherwise default settings, three regimens qualify: 17 × 3.05 Gy (23 days), 18 × 2.90 Gy (24 days) and 19 × 2.80 Gy (25 days). Both models predict a loss of 1.5–2.8 Gy₁₀ against HYPNO, and both changes stay within −3 Gy in all 15 settings, all of which are evaluable. With the default parameters, grid and limits, no regimen meets a nonnegative tumor BED change under both models. 17 × 3.05 Gy sits at HYPNO's acute BED (55.8 vs 55.9 Gy₁₀).

How to read it: agreement here is not two independent confirmations. Within each setting the models share α and the DD model is calibrated to the DI model at the anchor, and the settings share their other assumptions. These regimens meet a chosen modeled BED-loss allowance under both models and the selected settings, and they are 1–3 days shorter than HYPNO. The settings are not a probability sample; the Uncertainty tab gives intervals from the refit, and no probability of non-inferiority is calculated. Whether such a loss is acceptable depends on a clinical endpoint and margin (for HYPNO, 10 percentage points of locoregional control), which needs a dose-response relation. D18 is not fitted to outcomes. The Uncertainty tab gives a model-conditional tumor control illustration from the refitted α and an entered reference control rate, which does not establish clinical efficacy or non-inferiority.

### Parameter uncertainty (Bayesian refit to randomized trials, version 3.1)

The tumor models were refitted in Stan to 45 arms of 19 randomized trials of definitive fractionation, mainly in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma without cytotoxic chemotherapy (8,279 patients), re-extracted from the trial publications (data file refit_trials_2026_10_25c_main.csv in the repository). Each of the 21 within-trial comparisons has its own intercept, because the stage mix differs between trials, so the randomized contrasts inform the parameters. An arm-level random effect allows for overdispersion. An arm with mixed fraction sizes enters as the sum of its fractions, and overall time is the delivered time where the trial reports it. The likelihood is binomial on the reported control rate times the number of patients. This is an approximation, because most rates are Kaplan–Meier or cumulative-incidence estimates whose uncertainty differs from a binomial count. Priors: tumor α/β lognormal with 95% between 5 and 20 Gy; α and the repopulation rates broad lognormal; DI onset day normal (28 ± 10 days); background growth uniform 0.0046–0.021 per day.

What the refit shows:

- The DI model is informed by the data. Its daily repopulation loss λ/α is 0.63 Gy per day (95% interval 0.21–1.19) after an onset near day 37 (24–43), plus background growth of 0.28 Gy per day (0.11–0.48) throughout, in Gy at each draw's α/β. Every arm's control rate lies within its 90% predictive interval. This is an in-sample check with the fitted trial intercepts and arm effects, not prospective validation.
- A DD model fitted on its own did not sample reliably (divergent transitions in every variant). The calculator therefore uses DD calibrated to DI: for each DI draw, the DD onset constant is set so that both models give the same repopulation for 35 × 2 Gy in 47 days, and the DD rate is then fitted given that draw. Each DD draw is paired with the DI draw it was fitted on. The DD rate is 0.40 per day (0.12–1.03), narrower than its prior. A soft penalty discourages DD onsets before day 7. It is a preference, not a bound.
- The two models order 92% of the 55 pairs among 11 regimens (ten reference regimens and 35 × 2 Gy) in the same way. For 55 Gy in 20 fractions against 35 × 2 Gy, the median tumor BED change is −2.0 Gy under DI (80% range −4.3 to +1.0) and −3.3 Gy under DD (−5.9 to −0.6).
- Eight sensitivity analyses moved the DI median for that comparison between −2.7 and +0.3 Gy, and the DD median between −3.7 and −2.1 Gy. They added trials (ARTSCAN, three later trials, trials with a common chemotherapy policy in both arms, although planned or delivered exposure can still differ, as with four versus five weekly courses in HYPNO), kept only Kaplan–Meier rates, used the updated RTOG 9003 rates, halved every patient number, used the planned instead of the delivered overall time, or widened the intercept prior. The planned overall time moved the DI result most.
- Tumor α/β is not informed by these data: its posterior (median 10.6 Gy) is close to its prior.
- α is about 0.04 per Gy. It is an effective population-level slope given the endpoint, likelihood, priors, clock and arm-level data, not a measurement of intrinsic cellular radiosensitivity. Patient-to-patient variation may contribute, but the fit does not isolate the cause.

The Uncertainty tab applies 4000 draws to the evaluated regimen and the comparison together, so their difference carries the correlations between parameters. They come from 1000 DI posterior draws, each repeated with 4 paired DD draws, so the Monte Carlo error is approximate. It reports medians and 80% and 95% ranges of the tumor BED difference under each model, and shares of draws meeting the threshold and every toxicity limit, counted draw by draw. Toxicity parameters, which have no joint data, are drawn independently of the tumor parameters from stated priors (acute α/β 5–20 Gy, mucosal α 0.30–0.40 per Gy, doubling time 2–4 days, onset elapsed day 7–19; late α/β 2–4.5 Gy) or fixed at the sidebar values. Shares are rounded to 5 percentage points. The search under uncertainty uses 1000 draws and lists, per number of fractions, the regimen chosen by a rule stated before the search, with its neighbors on the dose grid, summarized with all draws. The refit, its diagnostics and the sensitivity analyses are documented in the repository (docs/development/2026_10_25_stages_BCDE_refit.md).

### Organs at risk (version 3)

Constraints are converted with the linear-quadratic model, as the HYPNO trial did: a constraint stated in 2-Gy fractions is an EQD2, and the physical limit in $n$ fractions is the dose $D$ with $D (D/n + \alpha/\beta) / (2 + \alpha/\beta)$ equal to that EQD2. HYPNO used α/β 3 Gy, and 2 Gy for the spinal cord and brainstem; QUANTEC gives 0.87 Gy for the cord, so the cord status is also checked from 0.87 to 2 Gy. Maximum-dose constraints are converted this way. A mean-dose limit is a physical mean in its source, so it is compared with the planned physical mean, and the source fractionation is stated. When the evaluated regimen's fractionation differs from the source's, the row is flagged: this is a physical source comparison only, the effect of changed fractionation is not validated, and it may understate the toxicity of hypofractionation. For QUANTEC the source is a conventional course with no single fraction count. The mean EQD2 is shown for illustration only, computed bin by bin when a dose-volume histogram is supplied.

Two libraries are offered. QUANTEC 2010 values (spinal cord 50 Gy, brainstem 54 Gy, parotid mean 20 Gy to one gland, submandibular mean 35 Gy, larynx mean 44 Gy, cochlea mean 45 Gy) [17-21]. These come from conventionally fractionated courses: the maximum-dose values are EQD2 and converted, and the mean-dose values are physical means, compared physically. The HYHOPE protocol's tables for 12, 15 and 20 fractions (section 4.1.4.2, pages 13-14) [6], converted from the table with the nearest number of fractions. HYHOPE does not state how its tables were derived, and they are not one exact LQ conversion: the EQD2 of each organ differs by up to 6.5% between its three tables. Every constraint in the library carries its source, page and quotation. Organs without a verified conventional constraint (mandible, constrictors, oral cavity, cervical esophagus, brachial plexus) can be assessed with the HYHOPE library only.

Statuses read "within", "within 5% of" or "exceeds" the converted constraint (maximum doses, under the stated α/β) or the source limit (mean doses). They are not statements of safety, and no complication probability is calculated. Dose-volume histogram files with patient name or identifier fields are refused. An upload is saved as a temporary file on the server, read into memory and deleted at once. It is not kept, logged or included in downloads. Real patient plans belong in the local version of the calculator.

### Calculation details

- **Smooth threshold.** Every $\max(0, x)$ is computed as $\ln(1 + e^{3x})/3$, as in the original calculator. This differs from the sharp threshold by less than $\ln 2/3$ = 0.231 days. It changes a BED by at most about 0.23 days times the daily repopulation loss ($\lambda/\alpha$ Gy per day), most when the overall time is close to the onset time: with the defaults, at most 0.23 Gy for the DI BED and 0.18 Gy for the early BED. For the DD BED it is up to about 0.3 Gy with the defaults over the default search range (up to 5 Gy per fraction) and 0.4 Gy up to 8 Gy, and larger with a small $\alpha$ or a large $\lambda_{DD}$. The threshold type is recorded in every export.
- **Overall time.** When computed from the calendar, $T$ counts calendar days from the first to the last fraction inclusive, so 35 fractions take 47 days from a Monday at 5 per week and 33 fractions take 38 days at 6 per week. A later first day or days without treatment lengthen $T$; a gap in a short course costs the same BED per day as in a long one once repopulation has started, but it is a larger share of the course. Twice-daily treatment is not covered. The tumor models use this count, as in [1]. Tumor parameters from sources that count elapsed days are converted when entered as presets (Hartley: onset day 22 elapsed = day 23 inclusive). A typed overall time cannot be shorter than the calendar allows.
- **Units of repopulation estimates.** Published "Gy per day" values are often in 2-Gy-equivalent (EQD2) units. In tumor BED at α/β 10 Gy they are 1.2 times larger: Withers' 0.6 Gy/day is 0.72 Gy₁₀/day and the HYPNO design's 0.65 Gy/day is 0.78 Gy₁₀/day [2, 10]. The tumor panel shows the current loss in both units.

### Default parameters and sources

**Tumor (DI and DD models).**

- Default values (version 2.3, scenario D18): $\alpha$ = 0.35 Gy⁻¹, $\alpha/\beta$ = 10 Gy, $\lambda_{DI}$ = 0.23 day⁻¹ (doubling time 3.0 days), $\lambda_{DD}$ = 0.40 day⁻¹, $T_k$ = 28 days, $g$ = 0. The DI loss is 0.66 Gy₁₀ per day after day 28, at the low end of the source estimates below (0.66–0.84 Gy₁₀/day), which makes shortening the course worth less. The DI values are within published ranges. The set is not fitted to outcome data, and its DD predictions are in tension with HYPNO (see Scenario D18 below). The version 1 defaults ($\alpha$ 0.2, $\lambda_{DI}$ 0.2, $\lambda_{DD}$ 0.5, a loss of 1.0 Gy₁₀ per day) remain available as a preset.
- Presets. A preset sets only the values listed for it. Where its source does not give a value, the preset uses a chosen value, marked below and in the provenance table; presets without a DD rate keep the DD maximum rate entered in the sidebar:
  - **Withers et al. [2]:** about 0.6 Gy per day in 2-Gy fractions after about 4 ± 1 weeks. The preset sets $\lambda_{DI} = \alpha \times 0.6\,(1 + 2/(\alpha/\beta))$ with $T_k$ = 28 days (a representative value of 4 ± 1 weeks), and follows the tumor α/β: 0.72 Gy₁₀/day at α/β 10 Gy, 0.84 Gy BED/day at 5 Gy. Withers gives the compensation rate and onset, not α: $\alpha$ = 0.2 Gy⁻¹ is this calculator's choice (the version 1 value), and the DI loss per day in BED does not depend on it.
  - **Hartley et al. [7]:** $\alpha$ = 0.3 Gy⁻¹, α/β 10 Gy, doubling time 3 days, onset day 22 elapsed (23 inclusive), 0.77 Gy₁₀/day.
  - **HYHOPE protocol [6]:** $\alpha$ = 0.35 Gy⁻¹, α/β 10 Gy, doubling time 3 days, $T_k$ = 21 days, 0.66 Gy₁₀/day.
  - **Scenario D18 (the default):** $\alpha$ = 0.35 Gy⁻¹, α/β 10 Gy, $\lambda_{DI}$ = 0.23 per day, $\lambda_{DD}$ = 0.40 per day, $T_k$ = 28 days. An analyst scenario chosen on 2026-10-18 so that DD repopulation rates and daily losses fall in ranges taken from DI-model studies. It is not a fitted set, and $\lambda_{DD}$ = 0.40 per day is a constructed value, not a published DD estimate. With it, HYPNO's test arm minus its control is −2.50 Gy₁₀ under DI and −5.79 Gy₁₀ under DD, 3.4% and 8.4% of the control arm's tumor BED (72.63 and 68.64 Gy₁₀). D18 is not fitted to outcomes, so it cannot say what these deficits mean for tumor control. The Uncertainty tab gives a model-conditional tumor control illustration from the refitted α and an entered reference control rate. It does not establish clinical efficacy or non-inferiority.
  - **Conditional illustration, not a prediction.** It needs three extra assumptions that neither the calculator nor the sources supply. (a) The normalized dose-response slope γ₅₀ estimated from the randomized hyperfractionation trials applies unchanged on this calculator's time-corrected EQD2 scale. (b) A logistic curve with that slope passes through HYPNO's control-arm 3-year locoregional control of 51.2%. (c) The populations, chemotherapy and endpoint timing transfer between the trials. Assumption (a) is a transfer, not a derivation: Stuschke and Thames define γ₅₀ on total (physical or isoeffective conventional) dose [3], and a normalized slope changes when a time correction is subtracted from the dose. Under (a)–(c), γ₅₀ = 3.2 (95% CI 1.6–4.8) [3] gives an illustrated loss of 13.4–25.0 percentage points for the DD deficit and 5.5–10.9 points for the DI deficit (at γ₅₀ 1.6 and 3.2), and the slope from RTOG 83-13 alone, 0.8 ± 0.4 [3], gives 6.8 (DD) and 2.8 (DI) points at 0.8 and 10.1 and 4.2 points at 1.2. For scale: HYPNO's upper 90% confidence limit for the hazard ratio, 1.30, corresponds under proportional hazards to 9.3 points at the control's 51.2% (a conversion, not an estimated limit on the absolute difference). The illustrated DD loss exceeds that figure for γ₅₀ above about 1.1, and the DI loss for γ₅₀ above about 2.7. So whether D18's predictions are compatible with HYPNO depends on assumptions the calculator cannot test. The illustration neither rejects D18's DD values nor validates its DI values. The calculation is in `docs/development/2026_10_19_v26/dose_response_illustration.R`.
- Clinical estimates of the tumor α/β for head and neck cancer are about 10 Gy, with wide uncertainty: 10.5 (6.5–29) Gy from hyperfractionation trials with 1.1–2.55 Gy fractions [3], summarized in [4]. Use at larger fractions is an extrapolation. A lower estimate of 6.4 Gy (2.7–10.2) comes from an unpublished overview cited in the HYPNO rationale.

- **Origin of preset values.** Direct = given by the source; conversion = derived from a source value; chosen = a representative or completing value set for this calculator; analyst = a constructed scenario.

**Early toxicity (acute mucosa).**

- Default values: $(\alpha/\beta)_e$ = 10 Gy, $\alpha_e$ = 0.35 Gy⁻¹, $T_{k,e}$ = 7 elapsed days, doubling time $T_{p,e}$ = 2.5 days, as proposed by Fowler et al. [5]. The HYHOPE protocol uses the same mucosal values [6].
- With these values, Fowler et al. place the acute tolerance zone at 59–61 Gy₁₀ for radiation alone [5]. It is an empirical gap between schedules reported as tolerable and intolerable, not a probability, and Fowler's own Table 1 marks 70 Gy in 35 fractions (53.1 Gy₁₀) as "near tolerance?". Fowler later described a 59–63 Gy₁₀ "Grey Zone" [9]. For concurrent chemotherapy, Fowler et al. suggest a decrement of possibly 3–5 Gy₁₀ [5]. The zone shown with chemotherapy (54–58 Gy₁₀) spans that range, and the Fowler limit in the search uses its lower edge. These zones apply only with Fowler's parameter values. Fowler et al. also advise that a regimen approaching the zone should first be tested in a small dose-escalation study observing acute reactions [5].
- With chemotherapy, the 53.1 Gy₁₀ of 70 Gy in 35 fractions is about 1 Gy₁₀ below the lowered zone (54 Gy₁₀), not well below it.
- **Acute toxicity observed with 55 Gy in 20 fractions** (55.9 Gy₁₀ modeled). HYPNO, randomized, 75% with weekly cisplatin: grade 3+ acute mucositis 50.9% (190/373) vs 54.7% (208/380) after 66 Gy in 33 fractions [10]. Jacinto et al., 20 patients with cisplatin: grade 3 mucositis 40%, median actual treatment time 29 days [11]. Miller et al., retrospective, older patients, almost all without chemotherapy: grade 3 mucositis (RTOG) in 72 of 113 completers at the end of treatment, 26% four weeks later [14]. Madhava et al., with carboplatin: grade 3 mucositis in 16 of 19, lasting 4 weeks or more in 3 [13]. These cohorts differ in population, grading and time point, so their rates cannot be compared to estimate a chemotherapy effect.
- A modeled acute BED at or below the comparison does not guarantee tolerability. HYHOPE dropped its 12-fraction level (44.4 Gy at 4 fractions/week, with weekly cisplatin) after an apparent increase in grade 2–3 mucositis and pain in a parallel postoperative trial. The protocol lists its modeled acute BED as 52 Gy₁₀ (51.3 Gy₁₀ with its stated parameters), against 51.4 Gy₁₀ for the 15-fraction level that continued, both computed by the protocol on its inclusive clock (19 days) [6]. On this calculator's elapsed clock the 15-fraction level is 52.2 Gy₁₀.
- Other estimates exist: $\alpha_e$ = 0.3 Gy⁻¹ [7], and a later refit on 108 schedules [8].

**Late toxicity.**

- Default value: $(\alpha/\beta)_l$ = 3 Gy, the conventional value for late normal-tissue effects [6, 9].
- Estimates for head and neck late effects vary with the endpoint. For grade 2+ late effects in one hyperfractionation trial the estimate was 3.3–4.0 Gy (confidence limits 2.2–6.7), depending on the assumed dose-response slope [3]. Spinal cord α/β is lower, about 0.87 Gy, so a single late α/β cannot stand in for the cord.
- A small decrease in modeled late BED has not been shown to be a clinical benefit. In the randomized altered-fractionation trials analyzed by Hartley et al., sparing of late mucosal toxicity was observed only where late mucosal BED fell by about 15% or more [7]. That is an aggregate observation in those trials, not a general threshold.

**Clinical context.**

- HYPNO (792 patients) compared 55 Gy in 20 fractions at 5 fractions/week with 66 Gy in 33 fractions at 6 fractions/week, not with 70 Gy in 35 fractions. Weekly cisplatin 35 mg/m² was given to 75%, and HPV status was not tested. At 3 years, locoregional control was 50.7% vs 51.2% (test minus control −0.5 percentage points, HR 1.098, 90% CI 0.93–1.30) and grade 3+ late effects 18.8% vs 20.2% (HR 0.926, 0.68–1.25). The test arm passed a 10-percentage-point non-inferiority margin for both [10]. HYPNO tested one schedule. Its result does not by itself establish any other schedule, and it does not select between the DI and DD models.
- HYHOPE (phase 1, 24 patients): favorable-risk HPV-positive oropharynx cancer, reduced elective volumes, 3 weekly doses of cisplatin 40 mg/m², designed against 60 Gy in 30 fractions as a de-escalation. 46.5 Gy in 15 fractions was the maximum tolerated dose. Grade 3 oral mucositis 5/24, any acute grade 3 toxicity 7/24, no late grade 3+ at a median of 26.6 months, 2-year locoregional recurrence 13.8% (95% CI 3.2–32%) [12]. It is not a comparison for unselected HNSCC.
- Placing a predicted ΔBED in Gy beside an observed difference in percentage points does not test a model. A fitted dose-response relation is needed for that.

### Limitations

- **Concurrent chemotherapy.** The chemotherapy checkbox only lowers the displayed Fowler tolerance zone for acute mucositis, and the Fowler early limit when that option is used in the search. No BED in this calculator, tumor, early or late, includes an effect of chemotherapy, although chemotherapy increases both tumor control and acute toxicity. A limit relative to the comparison compares radiation-only modeled BEDs, and differences in systemic therapy and its effects must be considered separately. Shorter courses usually deliver fewer cycles (HYPNO: 4 vs 5 weekly courses, 121 vs 149 mg/m² cumulative).
- **Screening surrogates.** The BED limits are screening surrogates, not validated safety constraints. Regimens with similar modeled acute BED can differ in observed toxicity (see the HYHOPE 12-fraction example), and the search ranks candidates by differences finer than any validation of the acute model.
- **Fowler's model** has no treatment volume or mucosal area, comes from pre-intensity-modulated data, assumes complete repair between fractions and applies to oral and pharyngeal mucosa only [5].
- Human papillomavirus (HPV) status of the tumor, tumor volume and stage are not modeled.
- Organ doses are assessed only where entered on the Organs at risk tab, against converted maximum-dose constraints and physical mean-dose limits. No normal-tissue complication probability is calculated, and the late BED at the prescription dose is only a coarse screen.
- Two or more fractions per day are not supported. Incomplete repair between same-day fractions would raise the BED.
- Overall duration is included, but individual gap timing and interruption effects are not modeled separately. The search builds regular once-daily weekday schedules.
- The default tumor parameters (Scenario D18) are not fitted to outcome data. The Bayesian refit is offered as a preset and on the Uncertainty tab, for review (see Parameter uncertainty above). The refit uses summary rates from trials of radiotherapy alone, without tumor HPV status.
- The tumor data cover 1.15–2.55 Gy per fraction. Tumor BEDs at larger fractions are extrapolations, and every table flags them.
