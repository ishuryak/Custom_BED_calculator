### How the tumor model was fitted

The fit used 45 arms or strata from 19 randomized trials of head and neck radiotherapy, with 8,279 analyzed patients. No fitted arm included concurrent cytotoxic chemotherapy. Some trials included nasopharyngeal cancer, and one (DAHANCA 6&7) gave the radiosensitizer nimorazole.

The trials reported local or locoregional control at two to five years. The list of fitted trials shows each published endpoint, time point and rate. The fit approximates these rates with rounded patient counts. Each of the 21 fitting units (a trial or a stratum within one) has its own baseline. Differences between randomized arms therefore provide the main information about how fraction size and treatment time affect tumor control.

Both models combine the effect of fraction size with tumor regrowth during treatment. In the fixed-onset (DI) model, accelerated regrowth begins gradually around day 37 (95% interval about 24 to 43 days). Well past onset it costs about 0.53 Gy EQD2 per day, on top of a background loss of about 0.23 Gy EQD2 per day from the start. Fraction-size sensitivity (α/β) is mostly set by its prior. The dose-dependent (DD) model could not be fitted reliably on its own. Its calculation therefore uses the DI fit and agrees with it by construction at 35 × 2 Gy in 47 days.

Refit support is the share of 4,000 paired parameter sets in which either tumor model meets the chosen tumor BED allowance. The fitted trials reach 2.55 Gy per fraction, so larger fractions are an extrapolation. The Uncertainty page shows the range of predictions under these assumptions.
